Introduction

An ANOVA is a linear model with a categorical predictor. It splits the variation into \(SS_{group}\), explained by the group means, and \(SS_{residual}\), left over within the groups. Each is divided by its degrees of freedom to give a mean square, and F is their ratio:

\[ F = \frac{MS_{group}}{MS_{residual}} = \frac{SS_{group}/(k-1)}{SS_{residual}/(N-k)} \]

In this workshop you will check your hand calculation in R, read the ANOVA table for a real experiment, and build on last week by checking the model’s residuals, including what one impossible value does to F.

The data

The data come from an experiment on the development of frogspawn. Newly laid frogspawn from a pond in the Italian Alps was divided between water containers kept at 13 °C, 18 °C or 25 °C, with about 20 containers at each temperature. The number of days until the eggs hatched was recorded for each container.

The residuals show vertical stripes because every observation in a group has the same fitted value, its group mean. The days are whole numbers, so the plots also show steps. Neither is a problem. Decide for yourself whether the plots show a clear departure from normality or from equal spread.

TipWhat about independence?

Independence cannot be checked from a plot, because it depends on the design. If several containers received spawn from the same clutch, their hatching times could be more alike than those of unrelated containers. The description of this experiment does not say how the spawn was divided, so you would ask, or state the assumption in your report.

Summary

A one-way ANOVA splits the variation into \(SS_{group}\) and \(SS_{residual}\). Dividing each by its degrees of freedom gives mean squares, and \(F = MS_{group} / MS_{residual}\) is signal divided by noise. A large F is evidence that the group means are not all equal, and \(R^2 = SS_{group} / SS_{total}\) gives the proportion of variation explained.

The F-test depends on the model’s assumptions: roughly normal residuals, similar spread in every group, and independent observations. Check them with residual and QQ plots. One impossible value can inflate the residual mean square enough to hide a very large effect, which is why you check the data before you analyse them and the model afterwards. A significant F says that at least one group mean differs; it does not say which ones.
