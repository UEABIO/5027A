The sampling distribution of the mean

A picture explainer

Start here

A large tub of beans. Every bean has a mass. Together they are the population.

Scoop once

Pick 20 beans at random. Weigh them. Compute the mean. You get one number.

Scoop again and again

Put the beans back, draw another 20, compute the mean again. Every scoop gives a different number, because different beans land in your hand.

Plot them all

Stack up all the sample means on a histogram. This is the sampling distribution. The dashed line is the true population mean.

Three things to notice
Centred on the truth The sampling distribution sits on top of the population mean. On average, the sample mean is correct.
Narrower than the raw data Averaging 20 beans dilutes extreme values. The spread of sample means is always less than the spread of individual beans.
Bell-shaped Even when individual bean masses are skewed, the sample means pile up into an approximately normal curve.
A statistic computed from a random sample is itself random. Everything that follows in this course — standard errors, confidence intervals, p-values — rests on this fact.
